HISTORY • ENGLAND • 1483

The Mystery of the Princes in the Tower

Two brothers. One tower. A secret more than 500 years old.

A Mysterious Disappearance

In 1483, two young English princes disappeared from the Tower of London. They were Edward V, aged 12, and his younger brother Richard, Duke of York, aged about 9.

Their disappearance became one of the most famous mysteries in English history. What happened to them? Why were they never seen again?

Edward V: The King Who Was Never Crowned

Born in 1470, Edward became king after the death of his father, Edward IV, in April 1483. However, he was only 12 years old, and his uncle Richard of Gloucester took control of the government.

Edward was taken to the Tower of London while preparations were being made for his coronation. The coronation never took place.

Richard, Duke of York: The Younger Brother

Born in 1473, Richard was Edward's younger brother. He joined Edward in the Tower of London in June 1483.

The two boys were seen there for the last time. After that, reliable accounts of their whereabouts disappeared.

A Timeline of Events

9 April 1483
King Edward IV dies.

May 1483
Edward V is taken to the Tower of London.

June 1483
His brother Richard joins him.

6 July 1483
Their uncle becomes King Richard III.

After the summer of 1483
The two princes are no longer seen. Their fate remains unknown.

Who Was Responsible?

Richard III is often suspected because he took the throne while his nephews could claim the crown. However, no definitive evidence proves who was responsible for their disappearance.

Other people have also been suspected. Historians continue to debate several theories.

A Mystery That Remains Unsolved

In 1674, the remains of two children were discovered in the Tower. They are sometimes believed to belong to the princes, but their identities have never been definitively established.

More than five centuries after their disappearance, one question remains: what really happened in 1483?
